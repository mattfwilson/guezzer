<objective>
Ship the control (D-01/D-02/D-03) and close the mechanism's three safety requirements: always
escapable (CHROME-03), removed from the a11y tree rather than translated (CHROME-04), and exactly one
resize with no camera snap (CHROME-05).

Purpose: this is the first plan in the phase that gives a user any way to **enter** chrome-hidden
state, so it is also the plan that must ship every way out. A phase boundary may not leave an
installed-PWA user able to strand themselves with force-quit as the only escape — at a show that also
costs the wake lock.

Output: `explore/ChromeToggle.tsx`, its `ExploreView` mount point, the control half of
`chromeToggle.test.tsx`, and `chromeResize.test.tsx`.

**A dedicated toggle button, not a canvas gesture (D-01).** `ExploreView` already spends single-tap on
node focus + chain-hop **and** on collapsing the filter panel (`filterOpen` is owned in `ExploreView`
specifically so a canvas tap can collapse it without a scrim). A third meaning for the same gesture is
a mis-fire waiting to happen while panning.
</objective>

## Planner decisions

**Icon (Claude's discretion, settled by `22-UI-SPEC.md` §Iconography):** `Maximize2` while chrome is
visible (tap = hide bars, the universal expand-viewing-area affordance) and `Minimize2` while hidden
(the exact inverse glyph, so the control reads as one toggle rather than two buttons). Probe P7
confirms both are live exports of the installed `lucide-react@1.23.0`. `Expand`/`Shrink` and
`ChevronsUpDown` were rejected — weaker recognition base, and the latter reads as "sort".

**No `aria-pressed` and no `aria-expanded` (D-05).** The accessible name carries the state by naming
the next action, matching the app's shipped `aria-label="Menu"` / `"Close menu"` idiom. `aria-pressed`
is the more correct toggle semantic in the abstract, but the app has no existing consumer of it and the
one control that must never confuse anyone is the wrong place to introduce a new idiom.

**No discovery affordance (D-06).** No hint, no once-per-build flag, no dismissal state. The glyph
carries it and the cost of missing it is zero — the app works exactly as it does today. The app already
carries one onboarding nag whose gating logic took two rounds to settle.

**Escape restores chrome; the OS back gesture is deliberately left alone (D-04).** Hijacking back means
pushing a history entry, and this app has hash-only navigation with no router; a spurious entry would
make back-out-of-the-app unpredictable mid-show. Phase 23's INSHOW-02 builds that mechanism once,
deliberately.

**Test-file paths:** `packages/app/test/chromeToggle.test.tsx` (extended here) and
`packages/app/test/chromeResize.test.tsx` — the `22-VALIDATION.md` paths, not `22-PATTERNS.md`'s
`test/explore/` suggestion.
